Ohio Housing Market Shifting Toward Buyers — Here's Where

The typical home in Ohio is now worth $255,750 — up from $247,286 a year ago. Prices are still climbing. But there are a lot more homes for sale right now — active listings are up 10% from last year. Buyer demand hasn't kept pace with the new supply.

Put simply: supply is outpacing demand. That's a reversal from the past several years, when demand outpaced supply. And it's turning a growing number of Ohio markets from seller's markets into buyer's markets. This builds on what I found back in July — Ohio price cuts just hit a 7-year high.

Buyers Are Gaining Ground in Ohio

Ohio's housing market is shifting toward buyers. Active listings are up 10% year-over-year. The pending ratio — a measure of buyer demand versus seller supply — has fallen to 0.76, the lowest since before the pandemic, giving buyers more negotiating room than at any point in the last five years.

One of the easiest ways to tell if buyers or sellers have the advantage is comparing how many buyers are active in the market to how many sellers are. I track this using the pending ratio — pending listings divided by active listings — pulled from Realtor.com data for all 88 Ohio counties. The number of homes going under contract tells you how many buyers are in the game. The number of homes for sale tells you how many sellers are in the game.

When buyers outnumber sellers, sellers have the advantage. When sellers outnumber buyers, buyers do.

Back in 2016, sellers badly outnumbered buyers — for roughly every 10 homes for sale, only about 4 were finding a buyer. Demand kept building through 2019, up to about 7 out of every 10. Then 2020 hit and everything flipped. Active listings collapsed while buyer demand stayed strong. For the first time, buyers outnumbered sellers.

By 2021 — the peak — there were about 16 buyers for every 10 homes for sale. That's when prices took off, jumping from $158,000 in 2019 to $198,000 by 2021, according to Zillow. Since that peak, the trend has moved the other way every single year. By 2024, buyers and sellers were roughly even. Today it's about 8 buyers for every 10 sellers — the weakest buyer turnout since before the pandemic, five straight years of decline.

What This Means If You're Buying or Selling in Ohio Right Now

If You're Buying

Head to my Ohio housing data page and look up the pending ratio for your county — or better yet, your specific ZIP code. Check the trend: is it going up or down?

  • Ratio near or above 1.0 — buyers still outnumber sellers. That's a seller's market. You can still negotiate, but expect to pay close to asking.
  • Ratio below 1.0, and dropping — sellers outnumber buyers. That's a buyer's market. Expect more listings to choose from, less competition, and more room to negotiate on price, repairs, or timeline.

If You're Selling

Same first step — look up your ZIP code's pending ratio on my Ohio housing data page.

  • Ratio near or above 1.0 — you have leverage. Expect decent buyer interest and multiple offers, as long as you price it strategically. An overpriced home still won't sell, no matter how strong the market.
  • Ratio below 1.0, and dropping — expect more competition from other sellers and more negotiating pressure from buyers. Pricing it right from day one matters more than it did two years ago.

This data is a compass, not an exact coordinate. County and ZIP-level numbers get you close, but hyperlocal analysis is what actually gets it right. A specific street, a specific school district, even a specific block can behave differently than the ZIP code average.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean when a housing market shifts toward buyers?

It means the number of active buyers is falling relative to the number of homes for sale. Homes sit longer, price cuts become more common, and buyers gain more room to negotiate.

Which Ohio counties have shifted the most toward buyers?

Butler, Montgomery, Lorain, Lake, and Summit have seen the biggest swings among Ohio's largest counties since the 2021 peak. See the full county breakdown.

Are Ohio's biggest counties still seller's markets?

No. All 10 of Ohio's most populated counties have shifted toward buyers since 2021 — Franklin, Cuyahoga, Hamilton, Summit, Montgomery, Butler, Lucas, Stark, Lorain, and Lake.

Are any Ohio counties still favoring sellers?

Yes — about a dozen, mostly smaller counties in Northeast Ohio (Medina, Portage, Wayne, Stark) and Northwest Ohio (Wood, Hancock, Defiance).

Is now a good time to buy a house in Ohio?

It depends on where — but it depends on you even more. In most of Ohio's biggest counties, buyers have more leverage than they've had in years. In a handful of smaller counties, sellers still have the edge.
