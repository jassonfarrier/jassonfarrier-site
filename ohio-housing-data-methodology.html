Ohio Housing Data Methodology

How every metric on the data page is defined, sourced, and calculated.

Every metric on the Ohio Housing Market Data page comes from a primary data provider — Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, PropStream, or the U.S. Census Bureau — covering all 88 Ohio counties, Ohio metro areas, and Ohio zip codes. Definitions below are sourced directly from each provider, followed by a plain-English explanation of what the number actually tells you about the Ohio housing market.

Zillow zillow.com/research/data →
SFH Home Value
Monthly · Single-Family · Mid-Tier · Smoothed & Seasonally Adjusted

Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI): a smoothed, seasonally adjusted measure of the typical single-family home value across a given region. It reflects the full distribution of home values — not just those that sold — and is reported for the middle tier of the market (33rd–67th percentile). Single-family only; condos and co-ops are excluded.

In plain terms: What a mid-range single-family home in this Ohio county or city is worth right now, based on Zillow's estimate of the entire housing stock — not just homes that happened to sell that month.
Median List Price
Monthly · Realtor.com · Active Listings

The median (middle) asking price across all active for-sale listings in the given area for that month, as reported by Realtor.com. Unlike ZHVI, this reflects what sellers are currently asking — not an estimate of underlying home value — and can move with the mix of homes listed in a given month.

In plain terms: The middle asking price for homes currently for sale in this part of Ohio. It moves with what sellers are pricing homes at today, which can shift faster than actual home values if the type of homes hitting the market changes month to month.
SFH Home Value YOY %
Monthly · Derived from ZHVI History

Year-over-year percent change in single-family ZHVI, calculated by comparing the current month's value to the same month 12 months prior.

In plain terms: How much single-family home values in this part of Ohio have gone up or down compared to exactly one year ago.
Condo Home Value
Monthly · Condo/Co-op · Mid-Tier · Smoothed & Seasonally Adjusted

Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI): a smoothed, seasonally adjusted measure of the typical condo or co-op value across a given region. It reflects the full distribution of condo values — not just those that sold — and is reported for the middle tier of the market (33rd–67th percentile). Condos and co-ops only; single-family homes are excluded.

In plain terms: What a mid-range condo in this Ohio market is worth right now, based on Zillow's estimate of the entire condo stock — not just units that happened to sell that month. Not all Ohio counties have condo data; rural markets with few condos may show N/A.
Condo Home Value YOY %
Monthly · Derived from Condo ZHVI History

Year-over-year percent change in condo ZHVI, calculated by comparing the current month's value to the same month 12 months prior.

In plain terms: How much Ohio condo values have gone up or down compared to exactly one year ago.
Realtor.com realtor.com/research/data →
Active Listings
Monthly · Count

Realtor.com defines active listings as properties listed for sale that are not yet under contract as of month-end. This count reflects available supply in the market at that point in time.

In plain terms: How many homes are sitting on the market available to buy right now in this Ohio county.
Active Listings YOY %
Monthly · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in the active listing count compared to the same month in the prior year.

In plain terms: Whether there are more or fewer Ohio homes for sale than this time last year.
Pending Sales
Monthly · Count

Realtor.com defines pending listings as homes with an accepted offer that have not yet closed as of month-end. Pending sales lead closed sales by approximately 30–60 days.

In plain terms: Homes under contract in this Ohio market — the best leading indicator of where closed sales are heading in the next 30–60 days.
Pending Sales YOY %
Monthly · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in pending sales count compared to the same month in the prior year.

In plain terms: Whether buyer demand in this part of Ohio is stronger or weaker than it was a year ago.
Pending Ratio
Monthly · Realtor.com · Housing Inventory Core Metrics

Realtor.com defines the pending ratio as the share of pending listings to active listings in a given market during the specified month.

In plain terms: How many Ohio homes are under contract relative to how many are sitting available. The further the ratio rises above 1.0, the more leverage sellers have. The further it falls below 1.0, the more leverage buyers have.
Pending Ratio YOY %
Monthly · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in the pending ratio compared to the same month in the prior year. Published by Realtor.com as part of their Housing Inventory Core Metrics.

In plain terms: Is the leverage in this Ohio market shifting toward sellers or buyers compared to a year ago — a rising number means sellers are gaining ground, a falling number means buyers are.
Days on Market
Monthly · Median Days

Realtor.com defines median days on market as the median number of days from a listing's active date to the date it went under contract, for all homes that went pending that month.

In plain terms: How fast homes are selling in this Ohio market — lower means a hotter market, higher means buyers have more time.
Days on Market YOY %
Monthly · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in median days on market compared to the same month in the prior year.

In plain terms: Whether Ohio homes here are selling faster or slower than they were a year ago.
Price Cut %
Monthly · Share of Active Listings

Realtor.com defines price reduced listings as the share of active listings that have received at least one price reduction from their original list price as of month-end.

In plain terms: How many Ohio sellers have had to drop their asking price — rising means sellers are losing pricing power.
Price Cut % YOY
Monthly · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year change in the share of active listings with a price reduction, compared to the same month in the prior year.

In plain terms: Whether Ohio sellers are cutting prices more or less than they were a year ago.
U.S. Census Bureau — ACS data.census.gov →
Annual Property Tax
Annual · 5-Year Estimate · Median

U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year estimates: median annual real estate taxes paid by owner-occupied housing units. The 5-year estimate pools responses over a 60-month period to produce reliable county-level data.

In plain terms: The typical property tax bill for an Ohio homeowner in this area — updated once a year when the Census releases new ACS estimates.
Property Tax YOY %
Annual · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in median annual property tax from one ACS 5-year release to the next.

In plain terms: How much the typical Ohio property tax bill changed here — reflecting local reassessments and levy changes.
Effective Tax Rate
Annual · Derived: ACS Median Tax ÷ ACS Median Home Value

Median annual property tax (ACS 5-year estimate, table B25103) divided by median home value (ACS 5-year estimate, table B25077) for that same geography and year, expressed as a percentage. Both figures come from the same Census ACS release — this is not compared against Zillow ZHVI. Covers Ohio counties (2009–2024) and Ohio zip codes (2011–2024, where ACS ZCTA-level data is available).

In plain terms: The actual tax burden as a share of home value — lets you compare tax load across Ohio counties and zip codes regardless of price level.
Tax Rate YOY %
Annual · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in the effective tax rate (ACS median tax ÷ ACS median home value) compared to the prior annual period.

In plain terms: Whether the tax burden relative to home value in this Ohio county went up or down compared to last year.

Est. Payment (20% Down / 3.5% Down)
Calculated Live In-Browser · Never Stored

A standard 30-year fixed monthly mortgage payment estimate, calculated fresh every time the page loads — never stored, since home values and rates change monthly. Principal & interest is calculated using the standard amortization formula on the home's Zillow ZHVI, financed at either 80% (20% down) or 96.5% (3.5% down) of value. PMI is intentionally excluded from both estimates — actual PMI varies too much by credit score, loan type, and lender to represent with one flat number without being misleading. Where county or zip-level property tax data is available (Census ACS, see above), monthly tax is added; Ohio metro areas and the statewide figure have no property tax source, so those payment estimates are principal, interest, and insurance only, clearly noted on the chart.

For illustration purposes only. The 3.5%-down figure excludes PMI. Consult a mortgage professional before making a purchase or investment decision.

In plain terms: What a monthly mortgage payment might realistically look like on a typical home in this area today, assuming a standard 30-year fixed loan.
Current Rate & Insurance Inputs
Updated Periodically · Mortgage News Daily & National Average

The current-value Est. Payment figures use a national average 30-year fixed mortgage rate (sourced from Mortgage News Daily's daily index) and a flat national average monthly homeowners insurance estimate. These are shared inputs applied uniformly across every Ohio county, zip code, and metro — not location-specific rate quotes or insurance premiums.

In plain terms: The rate and insurance numbers behind today's payment estimate are national averages, not what you'd personally be quoted — your actual rate depends on your credit, lender, and loan type, and your actual insurance depends on your specific home and coverage.
Historical Est. Payment Chart
Annual · 2007–2024 · Freddie Mac & NAR

The historical Est. Payment chart recalculates the same formula for each year using that year's actual national average 30-year fixed mortgage rate (Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey annual average) and that year's actual national average homeowners insurance premium (National Association of Realtors / industry annual estimate), combined with that year's actual Zillow home value and, for counties and zips, that year's actual Census ACS property tax figures. Coverage: counties 2009–2024, zip codes 2011–2024 (varies by zip, see Property Tax above), state and metro areas 2007–2024 (principal, interest & insurance only — no tax data source at those geography levels).

In plain terms: How the estimated monthly cost of owning a home in this area has actually changed over time — driven by rising home values, swinging mortgage rates, and climbing insurance and tax costs, not just one of those factors in isolation.
Redfin redfin.com/news/data-center →
Contract Cancellations
Monthly · Count · Redfin

Redfin defines home purchase cancellations as the number of home-purchase agreements that were cancelled in a given month. A cancellation occurs when a buyer and seller had a signed purchase agreement but the deal did not close.

In plain terms: How many buyers in this Ohio county backed out of a deal after going under contract — rising cancellations signal buyer hesitation, often tied to affordability pressure, inspection issues, or financing problems.
Cancellation Rate %
Monthly · Derived: Cancellations ÷ Pending Sales · Redfin

Cancellation rate is the number of cancelled home-purchase agreements in a given month divided by the total number of homes that went under contract (pending) in that month, expressed as a percentage.

In plain terms: The share of Ohio deals that fall apart after going under contract. A rising rate is a leading warning sign — it shows buyers are agreeing to deals but then walking away, which often precedes softening in closed sales and prices.
Cancellation Rate YOY %
Monthly · Year-over-Year Change

Year-over-year percent change in the cancellation rate compared to the same month in the prior year.

In plain terms: Whether Ohio deals are falling apart after going under contract more or less often than they were a year ago.
Delisted %
Monthly · Share of Listings · Redfin

Redfin defines "homes delisted" as the total number of unsold home listings that were removed from the market during a given time period. A home is counted as delisted when it goes off the market without selling or going under contract. Delisted % expresses this as a share of that month's active listings.

In plain terms: How many Ohio sellers gave up and pulled their house off the market instead of selling it or dropping the price. A rising number means more sellers in this area are choosing to wait rather than accept what buyers are offering — a sign sellers think the market will turn back in their favor, or that they can't sell at a price buyers will pay.
PropStream propstream.com →
Tax Delinquent
Monthly · Count · PropStream

PropStream defines tax delinquent properties as parcels with unpaid property taxes past their due date as recorded in county tax records. Data is aggregated at the county level from PropStream's nationwide property database.

In plain terms: The number of properties in this Ohio county where the owner has fallen behind on property taxes — a leading indicator of financial stress and potential future distress inventory.
Tax Delinquent %
Monthly · Derived: Tax Delinquent ÷ Total Properties

Tax delinquent property count divided by total property count for that county, as reported by PropStream, expressed as a percentage. Both figures come from PropStream's property database for the same reporting month.

In plain terms: The share of all properties in this Ohio county with delinquent taxes — normalizes the count so you can compare across counties of different sizes.
Pre-Foreclosure
Monthly · Count · PropStream

PropStream defines pre-foreclosure properties as parcels where the lender has filed a Notice of Default (NOD) or equivalent public pre-foreclosure filing because the borrower is behind on mortgage payments, but the property has not yet been foreclosed on or sold at auction. Data is aggregated at the county level from PropStream's nationwide property database.

In plain terms: The number of Ohio homeowners in this county who have missed enough mortgage payments that the lender has started the legal foreclosure process — but the home hasn't been repossessed or sold yet. Rising pre-foreclosure counts are one of the earliest warning signs of financial distress in a local housing market, showing up months before a property becomes bank-owned or heads to auction.
Pre-Foreclosure %
Monthly · % · PropStream

Pre-foreclosure count divided by total properties in the county, from PropStream's nationwide property database.

In plain terms: The share of all properties in this Ohio county currently in pre-foreclosure — normalizes the count so you can compare distress levels across counties of different sizes.
Underwater
Monthly · Count · PropStream

PropStream defines underwater (negative equity) properties as parcels where the estimated current mortgage balance exceeds the estimated current market value of the home. Data is aggregated at the county level from PropStream's nationwide property database.

In plain terms: The number of Ohio homeowners in this county who owe more on their mortgage than their home is currently worth. If they needed to sell, they'd have to bring cash to closing just to pay off the loan. Underwater homeowners are more likely to end up in foreclosure if they hit financial trouble, since selling isn't a clean way out.
Underwater %
Monthly · % · PropStream

Underwater property count divided by total properties in the county, from PropStream's nationwide property database.

In plain terms: The share of all properties in this Ohio county with negative equity — normalizes the count so you can compare across counties of different sizes.
